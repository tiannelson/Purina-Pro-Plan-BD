# GRASP Brief 1

**Goal**: I want to build a Skill that automatically pairs your BD meeting notes with live market/competitor research to produce one memo that connects them. The point is to stop losing the value of these meetings to notes nobody revisits, and to get that connection made the same way every time instead of you redoing the analysis by hand for each one.
**Resources**: I used 4 sets of Purina Pro Plan BD meeting notes (Retail Partner Review, Bright Mind Launch, Veterinary Channel Partnership, Sensitive Skin & Stomach Campaign) and uploaded them. I also had it do a Live web search to gather more information. A brainstorming session with Claude to sharpen search terms before any search was run: started at broad competitor keywords ("Hill's Science Diet"), then worked through common industry lingo (AAFCO, OTC vs. Rx, life stage formula, gut health/microbiome, premiumization, humanization, DTC/subscription) and paired each term with the competitor name, then trimmed the list down to three focus areas — health positioning, product & formulation, and market trends — before any search was actually executed.
**Autonomy Limits**: Claude decides on its own which search results count as relevant, how to phrase Market Signals, which entities to extract from notes, and how to draft the Implications/Recommended Actions connections. I make the initial keyword categories and the competitor pairing, which terms survive the trim, the scenario and company choice, and the final scope of any given memo.Claude should not be autonomous about inventing owners, dates, or causal links when the meeting notes don't contain them.
**Sign-off Point**: A human must review the Recommended Actions section against the source meeting notes, line by line, before anyone acts on it — specifically checking that every named owner, date, and action is actually traceable to something said in that meeting, not inferred from market research. This isn't a general "skim it once" check; it's targeted at the one section testing showed fabricates specifics under thin input. Secondary sign-off: any memo where a company-specific search came back empty and the memo leaned on general industry data instead should get a second look at the Implications section, since that's the other place testing found invented causal links.
**Proof**: Every Recommended Action can be pointed back to a specific line in the meeting notes. Every Market Signal is either a real, checkable citation or explicitly marked "no relevant signal found," never silently dropped. Implications never assert a causal link between a market signal and a meeting detail unless the notes actually support it.
